Primary Market Mechanics

Initial Public Offering:
From Private Enterprise to Public Ownership

An Initial Public Offering (IPO) is the institutional process by which a privately held corporation sells shares of its stock to the public for the first time, transitioning from unlisted private capital to publicly traded liquid securities on stock exchanges such as the NSE and BSE.

Fresh Issue of Shares

New equity units are minted. The capital raised flows directly onto the company's balance sheet to fund expansion, reduce corporate debt, or finance R&D.

Offer for Sale (OFS)

Existing owners (promoters, venture capitalists, early private equity) sell part of their holding. The capital goes to existing shareholders, not to the company's treasury.

The Lifecycle

How an IPO Works in the Market

From regulatory filing with SEBI to listing bell ring on the exchange floor.

Phase 1

Merchant Bankers & DRHP

The firm hires lead managers/investment banks and files the Draft Red Herring Prospectus (DRHP) with SEBI for review and public comment.

Phase 2

RHP, Price Band & Roadshows

Upon regulatory clearance, the Red Herring Prospectus (RHP) sets the floor price and cap price (price band), along with the minimum lot size for retail bidding.

Phase 3

3-Day Bidding & Book Building

Bids open across QIB, NII (HNI), and Retail categories. Retail investors bid using UPI ASBA mandate blocks through standard broker apps.

Phase 4

Allotment & Listing (T+3)

Under SEBI's T+3 framework, share allotment and unblocking occur within 2 business days, followed by listing and trading on the NSE and BSE by the 3rd business day.

Market Dynamics

Which Industries Lead the IPO Wave?

Domestic Transformation

What Domestic IPOs Bring to India

A vibrant domestic primary market bridges domestic household savings directly with productive industrial and digital infrastructure:

Capital Deepening: Channels retail Demat savings into institutional equity capital, reducing reliance on expensive debt.
Wealth Democratization: Enables retail participants to own shares in innovative enterprises at the early public growth stage.
Corporate Governance Rigor: SEBI listing obligations (LODR) enforce independent director oversight, quarterly earnings transparency, and audit scrutiny.
Global vs Domestic Route

Domestic Listing vs Listing Abroad

Historically, several technology unicorns incorporated offshore (flip structures) to target US/international listings. Today, India is reversing this trend:

Reverse Flipping to India: Founders are relocating legal domiciles back to India due to deep domestic liquidity and favorable retail valuation appetite.
Foreign Direct Investment (GIFT City): India's IFSC GIFT City enables dual-listing mechanisms, allowing global capital to tap Indian growth under international financial regulation.
Tax Base Retention: Listing on domestic exchanges retains transaction fees, STT, and capital gains taxes inside the domestic treasury.

Step-by-Step Procedure

How to Apply for an IPO Hassle-Free

Retail investors can bid up to ₹2,00,000 using standard UPI ASBA blocking.

Step 1: Place Bid via Broker

Open your stockbroker app during bidding hours (10:00 AM to 5:00 PM IST on open dates). Select the IPO, enter your bid lot quantity, select the Cut-Off Price (to maximize allocation probability), and enter your verified UPI ID.

Step 2: Approve UPI Mandate

Open your linked UPI app (Google Pay, BHIM, PhonePe). Look for a pending "Mandate / Autopay Request" from the registrar/exchange. Enter your UPI PIN to approve the block. Funds do not leave your bank account—they are simply blocked (ASBA).

Step 3: Allotment or Unblock

If shares are allotted, the blocked funds are debited and shares are credited to your Demat account before the listing date. If no allotment is received due to oversubscription, the bank unblocks your entire capital automatically.

Prudence & Caution

When is the Right Time & What to Check

Timing: When Should One Invest?

Many investors participate purely for short-term listing gains driven by speculative Grey Market Premiums (GMP). However, sustainable value investing treats an IPO as an entry into an unproven public business.

  • Bull Market Frenzy Warning: IPOs cluster during euphoric bull markets when promoters can command peak valuations. Approach late-cycle IPOs with heightened discipline.
  • Post-Listing Consolidation Alternative: If an IPO has rich valuations, prudent investors often wait for the mandatory anchor/promoter lock-in expiry (30 to 90 days) to see how the stock trades under market reality.

Pre-Investment Due Diligence Checklist

1. Issue Composition: Is the majority of the issue Fresh Capital (for company growth) or pure OFS (existing backers exiting)?
2. Valuation Multiple: Compare the IPO's P/E (Price-to-Earnings) and P/B multiples against established listed industry peers.
3. Corporate Governance & Litigation: Read Chapter 5 of the RHP for pending criminal or statutory tax litigation against promoters.
4. Cash Flow from Operations: Verify whether stated accounting profits translate into real operational free cash flow.
